TA Arena

Forty-two short games in seven worlds. Each world builds on the one before it.

What is Transactional Analysis?

Transactional Analysis, or TA, is a way of understanding how people talk to each other and why the same conversations keep going wrong. These games let you practise it, one short story at a time.

How the map works

Go down the map from world 1 to world 7. Each game leads to the next, and the sixth game in each world is the Sunday boss.

World 1 of 7 · Week 1 · Invisible

Hunger

Every person needs small signs that they are seen: a hello, a smile, a nod, even a cross look. Transactional Analysis calls these signs strokes. This world is about noticing them. Which ones do you give? Which ones do you take? What happens when you go without?

Why it comes here: This world comes first because nothing else makes sense until you can see the small moments between people. Strokes are the first thing to notice.

After this world you can tell a warm nod from a cold one, and notice what a day with almost none costs you.

Sunday boss: The Stroke Economy: break the five unwritten rules people use to hand out nods, and say who benefits from them

World 2 of 7 · Week 2 · Three Voices

Three Voices

Inside each of us there are three voices. The Boss repeats the rules we were taught as children. The Pilot works out what is true today. The Kid feels and wants, the way it did when we were small. In this world you learn to hear which voice is speaking.

Why it comes here: This world comes second because once you can see the moments between people, the next question is who is talking. The same words can come from any of the three voices.

After this world you can name which voice is talking, from the words, the tone and the posture.

Sunday boss: The Contaminated Adult: spot a Boss leak or a Kid leak in a sensible-sounding room, and bring it back to facts

World 3 of 7 · Week 3 · Volleys

Vectors

A vector is an arrow that shows a direction. In this world every line you hear is an arrow: it starts with one voice and lands on another. Some arrows land where they were aimed. Others cross over and land somewhere else. This world teaches you to draw the arrows and see where they go.

Why it comes here: This world comes third because you need the three voices to know where each arrow starts. Only then can you see where it lands.

After this world you can draw the arrow for any line, and say whether the reply matches it or cuts across it.

Sunday boss: The Salesman: find the sting in a friendly sales line, and answer both of its targets

World 4 of 7 · Week 4 · Traps

The Thesaurus

A thesaurus is a list of words that belong together. This world is a list of the repeating patterns that people fall into, and in this world we call them traps. Each trap has a hook, a switch and a payoff. You learn to spot which trap is running.

Why it comes here: This world comes fourth because once you can follow a line and its reply, you can notice when the same exchange keeps coming back. A repeating exchange is a trap, and this world gives each one a name.

After this world you can tell a trap from real talk by listening for the hook, the switch and what the trap pays.

Sunday boss: Why Don’t You, Yes But: step out of a loop of yes-buts without leaving the room

World 5 of 7 · Week 5 · Underneath

Underneath

Under many traps is a feeling that does not fit the moment: a sting, a grudge, a guilt that never goes away. Some people save these feelings like stamps, to cash them in later. In this world you learn to tell a real feeling from a stand-in one.

Why it comes here: This world comes fifth because you can only find the real feeling once you have spotted the trap that is covering it.

After this world you can tell a real feeling, which finishes something, from a stand-in feeling, which never does.

Sunday boss: The Discount Matrix: see the kind-sounding line that keeps a trap going, and say what a real step would look like

World 6 of 7 · Week 6 · The Plan

The Plan

Many people live by a plan they made as children. It tells them what they must do, what they must never do, and how their life should end. When the same traps keep coming back, the plan is usually behind them. In this world you learn to read the plan.

Why it comes here: This world comes sixth because the traps add up to a plan. You need to know the traps before you can see the plan they build.

After this world you can hear the rule running under a line, and read the plan a life story is following.

Sunday boss: The Script Matrix: place a life story’s rules and plan on one board, and suggest a new line that can be acted on this month

World 7 of 7 · Week 7 · Your Own Person

Autonomy

Autonomy means being free to choose your own moves, instead of running the moves you learned as a child. In this world you learn to help without rescuing, to make clear agreements, to say yes with a safety net, and to pick the response that is truly yours.

Why it comes here: This world comes last because once you can see your plan, you can change it. The worlds before this one help you see. This one helps you act.

After this world you can help without rescuing, make clear agreements, and keep a hard conversation honest.

Sunday boss: After Games, What?: keep a candid conversation real for six turns, without rescuing or blaming anyone
