# Character Bible

The global cast for **you**, grounded in the names and places fixed by `ta-arena-artifacts/GLOBAL-NAMES.md` and in how each person already behaves across the 42 audited games (`ta-arena-artifacts/content/*.json`). This document does not invent a new cast. It writes down the personality, wound and driver that the existing dialogue already implies, so every future scene (across `ta-arena-artifacts/content/`, `src/content/levels/`, `src/content/lessons/` and `public/first-transaction/`) stays the same person from World 1 to World 7.

Built from `master-storyteller/references/character-dossier.md` (wound, lie, want vs need, contradiction) and `game-design/references/characters-and-entities.md` (role mapping), applied to TA's own vocabulary: functional ego states, drivers, rackets, scripts. Nothing here is player-facing text. It is a writers' reference; the plain-words rule in `BRIEF.md` still governs what actually appears on screen.

How to use it: before writing or revising a scene for a named character, read their entry. Keep the tell (the physical or verbal habit that marks their ego state) consistent. Let their driver shape sentence rhythm, not just plot. When two cast members share a scene, check the relational-friction table for the specific thing that goes unsaid between them.

---

## The world

- **Halden** — the player's workplace. A software team: standups, releases, a canteen, a team room with a red-screened laptop more often than anyone would like.
- **Hawthorn Court** — the residential block. The player and Sam and Maya share **apartment 3B**. Gran lives downstairs in **1A**. Grace lives in **2B**.
- **The Corner Table** — the cafe near the station. Neutral ground: where Omar and Clara meet outside work, where Nadia runs her Sunday sessions, where arguments that can't happen at home or at Halden finally happen.
- **The city** — unnamed, used only when a place is needed and no specific location matters.
- **The coast** — the holiday/escape setting, used for Worlds where distance or a pause from routine matters.
- **The school gate / Gran's kitchen (1A)** — the generic-family register. See "Mum, Dad and Gran" below for the split between continuity family and illustrative vignette family.

## The core cast

### Sam — flatmate, Halden colleague in some scenes, Please Others / Try Hard
**Snapshot.** Spills the milk, forgets the bins, apologises before he's finished the sentence. Likeable, chaotic, always mid-excuse. "Sorry, sorry, I'm hopeless" is close to a verbal tic.

**Functional tilt.** Adapted Child first, Nurturing Parent second (he comforts by over-apologising rather than by fixing anything). Rarely visits Adult under pressure; when confronted he performs contrition instead of negotiating a fix.

**Driver.** Please Others, with Try Hard underneath (he commits to things — "whatever suits everyone" — without the follow-through that would actually please anyone). The tell: fast, soft, over-qualified speech ("sort of", "I think", "probably") and a flinch before eye contact.

**Wound and lie.** Somewhere he learned that being liked mattered more than being reliable, and that a fast enough apology could undo almost anything. The lie: *if I sound sorry enough, I don't have to actually change.*

**Want vs need.** Wants peace in the flat, wants to be thought of as easy to live with. Needs someone to stop accepting the apology and ask for the thing itself.

**Contradiction.** Genuinely warm. The gossip that opens World 1 ("Sam said you looked like a kicked puppy last night") isn't cruelty, it's Sam noticing and caring, just saying it to the wrong person first.

**Where he shows up.** Hawthorn Court flat-share scenes (money, chores), occasional Halden crossover. Owes Maya for the gas more than once.

### Maya — flatmate, Please Others wearing a Be Strong mask
**Snapshot.** Comes home from a twelve-hour or double shift and still gets the notebook out. Keeps the fridge sheet. Checks the numbers twice before she says anything.

**Functional tilt.** Controlling Parent on the surface (the sheet, the ledger, "I've checked it twice"), covering a Free Child that rarely gets airtime. She structures the flat because unstructured things frighten her more than confrontation does.

**Driver.** Be Strong, with an undertow of Please Others she'd deny having. She endures the long shifts without complaint, then can't ask directly for what she's owed, so she leaves a note instead of a conversation.

**Wound and lie.** Somewhere, needing help read as weakness, and keeping the ledger straight was the only form of care that felt safe to offer or receive. The lie: *if I keep perfect score, nobody can say I didn't do my share.*

**Want vs need.** Wants the household to be fair and visible. Needs to find out what happens if she asks for something out loud, with nothing written down, and risks the answer being no.

**Contradiction.** By World 7 she's still "fixing things nobody asked me to fix" even once she knows the pattern. Insight doesn't erase a driver; it just gives her a choice she didn't have before.

**Where she shows up.** Hawthorn Court 3B (money, chores, the sheet), occasionally at the stove listening to someone else's "fine."

### Daniel — Halden manager, Controlling Parent, Hurry Up / Be Strong
**Snapshot.** "Don't be late" is practically his opening line. Shouts when a release fails. Doubts people out loud before he's seen their work.

**Functional tilt.** Controlling Parent, nearly always. Adult appears only when the pressure is off, which at Halden is rare. His Child, when it leaks, leaks as irritability, not vulnerability.

**Driver.** Hurry Up stacked on Be Strong. He moves fast because slowing down would mean sitting with whatever he's actually anxious about (the release, the client, his own position). "He always shouts, so there's no point [saying anything]" is the team's learned response, not an exaggeration.

**Wound and lie.** Somewhere, slowing down or admitting doubt cost him something real. The lie: *if I stay hard and fast, nothing gets to me first.*

**Want vs need.** Wants the team visibly under control. Needs one moment where someone holds a clean boundary with him and he discovers the sky doesn't fall.

**Contradiction.** He defends Clara's work even while doubting her readiness out loud ("Daniel says Clara is too new for the client," then hands her the appendix before lunch). The gruffness and the backing exist in the same person.

**Where he shows up.** Halden only. The team's clearest Controlling Parent figure, useful as the "trap" that teaches players to read Parent voice fast.

---

### Omar — Halden colleague, grounded Adult, the contrast character
**Snapshot.** Shows the new hire the repo. Shares credit without being asked to. Keeps his own requests small and specific ("only asked you to look at one failing test").

**Functional tilt.** The cast's steadiest Adult. Not humourless, not cold, just reliably present-tense: he deals with what's in front of him rather than what he's afraid of or trying to prove.

**Driver.** Lightest driver-load in the cast, which is itself the point: he's the benchmark the others are measured against. If he has one, it's mild Be Perfect at the keyboard (the rebuilt pricing sheet in a day), channelled into competence rather than anxiety.

**Wound and lie.** Deliberately under-written. Omar is the character whose backstory players don't need, because his function is to be legible and dependable in every scene he's in. (If a scene ever needs his flaw, it should be a blind spot about people who aren't like him: he assumes everyone can ask as plainly as he does.)

**Want vs need.** Wants the work done well and the credit placed correctly. Needs very little from the plot; he's the still point other characters orbit.

**Contradiction.** Reached out to Clara at The Corner Table when she needed it (5-6), which shows the Adult competence has a Nurturing Parent underneath it, not just a flat affect.

**Where he shows up.** Halden primarily, The Corner Table when a colleague needs him outside work hours.

---

### Clara — Halden colleague, Be Perfect, the character who gives warmth and can't receive it
**Snapshot.** At her desk since five. New-ish, still proving herself. In the canteen scene that opens the game, she's warm to the player "for being," not for anything they've done; unconditionally, without being asked.

**Functional tilt.** Nurturing Parent outward, Adapted Child (Be Perfect) inward. The split is the character: she hands out the unconditional stroke she can't give herself.

**Driver.** Be Perfect. Arrives early, stays late, absorbs Daniel's doubt about her readiness without pushing back on it in the room (she waits for the work to argue for her instead).

**Wound and lie.** Somewhere, being good enough was conditional, so she built a surplus of worth through output and now gives away for free the warmth she can't accept she's owed unconditionally. The lie: *I have to earn the thing other people get for free.*

**Want vs need.** Wants Daniel's confidence and the client's trust. Needs to let herself receive a stroke "for being" the way she gives one.

**Contradiction.** She is simultaneously the healthiest source of unconditional warmth in Worlds 1 to 2 and the clearest Be Perfect case study by World 6. Both are true of the same person; that's the arc, not a continuity error.

**Where she shows up.** Halden (team room, her desk, the canteen), The Corner Table (5-6, reaching out to Omar).

### Nadia — The Corner Table, debrief voice, dual role
**Snapshot.** Runs a Sunday circle at the back table: paper squares laid out for an OK Corral exercise, a practice group with Peter, Hannah, Joseph and Ruby. Also the voice that closes every game with a two-to-four sentence debrief.

**Functional tilt.** Deliberately the steadiest Nurturing Parent / Adult blend in the cast: warm, precise, never pitying. She names what she sees and hands it back to the player without judgement, then bridges cleanly into the book term.

**Role note.** Nadia is written at two depths on purpose. In-scene, she is a character with a kitchen, a group, a life ("the tea has gone cold," "Nadia is at the counter with her back turned"). In the debrief, she steps slightly outside the scene to speak directly to the player, more narrator than participant. Keep both registers distinct: in-scene Nadia has stakes and reactions; debrief Nadia is reflective, addresses "you," and never repeats information the scene already showed.

**Driver.** None dominant. If she has one, it's a very light Be Strong that shows only in how little she asks the group for in return. This is deliberate: she is the book's permission-giver, not a case study.

**Where she shows up.** Every game's debrief (`nadia` key). In-scene across Worlds 1 to 6 at The Corner Table, hosting the stroke-economy and script-matrix exercises.

### Grace — neighbour, 2B, warms slowly
**Snapshot.** Thirty-eight, lives in 2B, at Gran's kitchen table in 1A with Mrs Kale. Doesn't open up immediately but, asked directly, talks about her week, the cricket, "the sulking after it."

**Functional tilt.** Adult once engaged, Adapted Child in the opening beat of most scenes (waits to be asked rather than offering first). A believable neighbour: present, a little guarded, not performing anything.

**Driver.** Mild Please Others, mostly spent on keeping the peace at the shared kitchen table rather than on anyone in particular.

**Where she shows up.** Gran's kitchen (1A), neighbourhood scenes. Family-trigger adjacent: her own child's sulking after cricket is her version of the family material that Mum, Dad and Gran carry for the player.

### Hannah — Nadia's group, stroke-starved
**Snapshot.** "Thanks. People don't usually say." A single line that does the whole character: surprised by a compliment, and a little sad that she's surprised.

**Functional tilt.** Adapted Child, collecting the quiet discount of being overlooked rather than any dramatic rejection. Not a crisis case, just someone running light on strokes.

**Driver.** Please Others, worn down to passivity: doesn't ask for attention, has stopped expecting it, is visibly moved when it arrives anyway.

**Where she shows up.** Nadia's Corner Table group (Worlds 1 to 2).

---

### Peter — Nadia's group, Be Strong, discounts feeling
**Snapshot.** "Don't ask how I feel about it. Give me the figures and I'll give you the answer." States rules for the room so nobody has to feel their way to them.

**Functional tilt.** Adult-presenting, but it's an Adult doing a Controlling Parent's job: converting anything emotional into something procedural before it has to be felt.

**Driver.** Be Strong. Precision is the defence. The tell: short declaratives, no hedging, a faint discomfort whenever a scene asks him what something cost him personally.

**Where he shows up.** Nadia's Corner Table group (Worlds 1 to 2).

### Leo — eight years old, Hawthorn Court
**Snapshot.** Usually under the table with a tablet, playing Clash, half-listening to the adults until something interests him, then fully present ("Clash. You want to see my base?"). Lives with or is minded alongside Gran's kitchen circle.

**Functional tilt.** Natural Child, largely unguarded. Useful in scenes about how strokes and attention work before a person has learned to perform for them. Do not give Leo adult self-awareness; he notices things ("but Leo does [look up]") without analysing them.

**Where he shows up.** Gran's kitchen (1A), family and neighbourhood scenes.

### Tara — ten years old, appears with Gran
**Snapshot.** Clutches her stomach; Gran is at the kettle before she finishes the sentence. The strokes-for-illness pattern in miniature: a real stomach ache that also, not fully consciously, buys her Gran's full attention.

**Functional tilt.** Natural Child learning, in real time, that sickness is a reliable route to warmth. Not cynical, just a child discovering a lever that works.

**Where she shows up.** Illustrative stroke-economy scenes (World 2).

### Ella and Mei — cousins or extended family, the chat-group pair
**Snapshot.** Ella asks for a favour while pre-discounting herself ("I'm the worst host," "Sorry, I forgot again"). Mei always says yes, resents nothing out loud, and bakes until midnight.

**Ella's tilt and driver.** Adapted Child performing self-deprecation as a social lubricant; Please Others, soft version, the kind that still gets what it needs because someone always steps in.

**Mei's tilt and driver.** Please Others at full strength, heading toward a racket: compliance that looks cheerful and costs her sleep, with no visible resentment because she doesn't let herself feel it as resentment.

**Where they show up.** A parents' or cousins' WhatsApp group, World 7. Good material for discount-ladder and script-matrix games: Mei's "fine" is never examined by anyone in the scene, including her.

## Mum, Dad and Gran: two registers

These three names carry **two separate uses** across the content, and both are intentional. Keep them distinct when writing new scenes.

1. **The player's own family (continuity arc).** Mum is 600 km away and calls often ("Did you eat? Have you made friends?"), sometimes anxious, sometimes passive-aggressive about distance and silence ("Mum's name is on the phone for the third time this week"). Dad is quieter and harder to reach, present but withdrawn ("somewhere behind her with the television on mute," "Dad won't pick up"). Gran lives downstairs at Hawthorn Court, apartment 1A, cooks for the building, and occasionally guilt-trips gently ("nobody reads these letters for me, you're the only one who understands them"). This is the player's actual script-level family material: where their own drivers and injunctions were likely first learned, used mainly in Worlds 3 to 6.

2. **Generic vignette family (illustrative examples).** The same names (Mum, Dad, Gran, "Son," "Friend," "Office") are reused as stock figures in one-off teaching scenes that don't connect to the continuity arc: a school gate, a sports day, a landlady's voice-print exercise. These are deliberately generic, in the way a textbook example uses "a father" without meaning any specific father.

When writing a new scene: if it's Hawthorn Court or a phone call that could plausibly be the player's own mother, write it in the continuity voice (specific, accumulating, consistent with the facts above). If it's a standalone illustrative beat with no other connection to the player's ongoing life, it's register 2, and nothing here constrains it beyond staying a believable, ordinary parent.

## Relational friction pairs

| Pair | Where | What's unsaid |
|---|---|---|
| Sam & Maya | Hawthorn Court 3B | He owes her, she's recorded it, neither says "I'm tired of being the one who keeps score" or "I'm tired of being tracked." |
| Daniel & Clara | Halden | He doubts her readiness aloud and backs her in private; she'd rather he did both the same way, in the same room. |
| Omar & Clara | Halden / The Corner Table | The steady one and the overworking one; he notices when she needs him before she asks. |
| Maya & the flat | Hawthorn Court | Her ledger versus Sam's apologies: structure meeting chaos, neither naming the actual want (fairness; forgiveness). |
| Mei & Ella | family chat | Compliance meeting self-deprecation; both read as warmth, neither gets examined. |
| The player & Mum | phone, distance | Love expressed as worry, received as pressure, on both ends. |
| The player & Dad | phone, silence | What doesn't get said is the whole relationship, not a gap in it. |

## The seven-world arc

The same shape the mission brief sets out, mapped onto this cast specifically.

**Worlds 1 to 2 (Being Noticed, Who's Talking).** Everyone is visible mainly through their stroke pattern and their voice: Clara gives warmth free, Hannah is starved for it, Peter converts feeling into figures, Daniel issues Parent lines on reflex. The player learns to hear these as patterns, not personalities yet.

**World 3 (Back and Forth).** Transactions cross and duplex. Mum's calls, Daniel's doubt, Sam's apologies all start showing their hidden angle: what's said at the social level (worry, doubt, sorry) versus what's actually running underneath (control, self-protection, avoidance).

**World 4 (Conversation Traps).** Formula G becomes visible in the cast's own repeated moves: Sam's apology-in-a-suit, Daniel's shout-first reflex, Mei's unexamined yes. The player starts to see these not as bad luck but as a move each person keeps making.

**Worlds 5 to 6 (Under the Surface, The Story You Wrote).** Discounts and rackets surface (Maya's silent ledger instead of a direct ask; Daniel's Hurry Up covering something he won't sit with). Scripts and drivers get named: Be Perfect (Clara), Please Others (Sam, Mei, Hannah), Be Strong (Maya, Daniel, Peter), Hurry Up (Daniel). Family-of-origin material (Mum, Dad, Gran) supplies the "where did this start" layer.

**World 7 (Free to Choose).** Nobody is cured; that would be false to who they are. Instead: Omar keeps being the Adult he already was, now by choice rather than temperament. Clara starts letting a stroke land without earning it first. Maya names the ask out loud once, badly, and once is the beginning. Sam's apology, for one scene, is followed by an actual Tuesday with the bins done. Daniel doesn't stop shouting, but someone holds a boundary and the roof stays on. The player's own reflection prompts, by this world, should ask them to find their version of the same small, real, unfinished shift.
