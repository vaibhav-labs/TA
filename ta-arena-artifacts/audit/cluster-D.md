## Findings that affect the design table

- The 42-game table says 7.1 uses "Steiner's three Rescue rules". SPL has no verbatim list of three. Ch. 19 closes with a two-part summary (SPL p. 232), lists sub-rules A to E under point 1 (SPL p. 230), and defines the rescuer role as helping on request without demanding equal effort, or "imposing help without a request" (SPL p. 230). Ch. 16 (SPL p. 204) names three ways rescues happen: doing what you don't want to do, one person putting in more than 50% of the effort, and not asking for what you want. The game now says exactly this: two signs in Steiner's summary, plus the no-help-without-a-request rule, plus the not-asking point in the debrief.
- The table says 7.2 validates "Berne's four requirements". The four requirements are Steiner's (SPL ch. 20), borrowed from contract law, and TA Today ch. 26 names them "Steiner's four requirements" on its contents page. GPP and TAP contain no such list (searched "contract" in both). The "cooperative-contract clauses" are Steiner's five cooperation rules in SPL ch. 25 (SPL p. 280 to 284: no scarcity, equal rights, no power plays, no secrets, no rescues). Not used in 7.2; ch. 20 was enough.
- The bible's plain-word gloss for potency ("the backbone", a reason the person can do it, as 7-3 first had it) does not match SPL. Steiner puts potency in the giver (SPL p. 251). Corrected in 7-3 while keeping the plain word "backbone" for the giver's strength.
