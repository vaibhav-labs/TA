Plain words first: these are grudge coupons. Gran keeps hers in her head and Grace keeps hers in a drawer. The books call them trading stamps, a feeling saved up instead of said, to be traded in later on someone else or on yourself. Gran could have said her wrist was sore on Wednesdays. Grace could have said she wanted to be asked. Either would have left nothing to pay out on the stairs.
Answering each complaint sounds fair, but it treats every line as a single day. Gran and Grace would each hear that the day was dealt with. The coupons would stay in the book, and the cash-in would still come.
Agreeing and moving on keeps the count hidden. The stamps stay put, and the feelings are agreed with and never asked about.
